# SideStep Script Kit v1.1

For AI creator accounts posting job-search content. Use with any caption, storytime, slideshow or talking video. Version 1.2, Oct 16 2026 (claims confirmed by Eric and aligned to getsidestep.com; product hooks and two vault hooks updated to match `claude/sidestep-social-strategy.md`).

How to use it:
1. Pick a hook from the vault (or the strategy doc's hook bank).
2. Rewrite it in the character's voice (voice cards below).
3. Pull real wording from the buyer-language bank.
4. If the post mentions SideStep, stay inside the claims sheet.
5. Keep the 80/20 mix: about 80% pure value and relatable posts, 20% soft product mentions.

## 1. Claims sheet (what we can say about SideStep)

Confirmed by Eric on Oct 16, 2026 and checked against getsidestep.com (the base for all marketing). Nothing outside this list goes into a script. When the site changes, this sheet changes with it.

What it is:
- A Chrome extension for job seekers. Desktop Chrome only, Google sign-in, 18+. Free to install; the $99 pass unlocks it. No free version.

What it does:
- Sets up your profile from your resume (100+ autofill fields). No typing your work history.
- Job match on every posting: compares your resume to the job so you see how you fit before you apply.
- Resume tailoring, "surgical, not a rewrite": points to real things you've done and where to add them, and what to polish or remove for this job. You decide and approve every change. It never rewrites your resume and never invents experience.
- One click fills the application on the page you're on. Works on LinkedIn, Indeed, other job boards, company career pages and ATS application pages. (The site recommends applying on company websites.)
- It does not apply for you. You review and submit every application yourself.

Price:
- $99 once for 365 days. No subscription. No auto-renew.

How to say it (approved phrasings, matching the site):
- "everything those expensive job search tools do, for $99 once"
- "it shows you how you match before you apply"
- "surgical, not a rewrite: it points to real things you've done and where to add them"
- "it never makes up experience"
- "one click fills the application, you review and hit submit"
- "$99 once, a full year, no subscription, no auto-renew"
- "cut your job search time by 90%" and "get hired faster" (the site's hero; Eric holds the data behind both, Oct 16)

Never say:
- Any promise of interviews, offers or callbacks, or a "% more interviews" number. The site's own "get hired faster" is the one approved hiring line; no other outcome claims.
- Any time-saved number other than the site's 90%. "Long applications take 20 to 30 minutes by hand" is fine as the size of the pain.
- That it "beats" or "tricks" applicant tracking systems.
- That it writes or rewrites your resume, or writes experience you don't have.
- That it applies for you or auto-submits.
- That it detects ghost jobs or flags salary ranges (not shipped; ghost jobs are a market stat only).
- That your data never leaves your computer or that it's "local-first." Per the site: your profile is stored in your browser; resume text is sent to an AI provider, not kept, not used for training, not sold. Say only that.
- That it works on a phone.
- "Your whole search is covered" (access is 365 days).
- Fake results from a character ("I got hired in 2 weeks with SideStep").

Market stats the site uses (use these first so content matches the landing page; cite on a slide or in the caption):
- About 254 applicants per job posting (Greenhouse CEO, July 2026).
- More than 1 in 4 unemployed Americans have searched 6+ months (BLS, Aug 2026).
- Hiring at its lowest rate since 2010 (BLS JOLTS, July 2026).
- About 1 job opening per job seeker, down from about 2 in 2022.
- 1 in 5 postings may be ghost jobs, 18 to 22% (Greenhouse platform data).
- 53% of job seekers were ghosted by an employer in the past year (iHire, 2026).
- The average job search lasts about 26 weeks.

Safe framing: tips and observations ("now she checks how she matches before she applies"), not results claims.

Where the link goes: in the post caption or bio, never on the video. Each account gets its own tracked link or promo code.

## 3. Hook vault (50)

Grouped by type. Every hook opens on conflict, a surprise or a question. Never "hey guys" or "today I want to talk about." Never imply the viewer is doing it wrong; the system is the failure.

### Relatable pain (storytime openers)
1. My friend applied to 200 jobs and the only email she got was a newsletter.
2. They ghosted her after the final round, then reposted the job as "urgently hiring."
3. Entry level. 5 years experience. Pick one.
4. Recruiter called my roommate "a perfect fit" and then never called again.
5. My coworker did a 6-hour unpaid "take-home project" for a job that didn't exist.
6. Got rejected at 2:14am. Who is up rejecting people at 2:14am?
7. Five rounds of interviews for a role that pays less than my old one.
8. She got "overqualified" and "underqualified" in the same week.
9. The salary range said "competitive." Competitive with what?
10. My friend's resume went into a black hole and came back as a LinkedIn ad.

### Insider secret
11. Most resumes are rejected before a human ever reads them. Here's why.
12. A recruiter told me the real reason they never called back.
13. That job posting you applied to might not even be real.
14. The first 48 hours after a job posts matter more than your resume.
15. Hiring managers spend about 7 seconds on your resume. Here's what they look at.
16. Why "Easy Apply" is the hardest way to get a job.
17. The words in the job posting are the cheat code. Nobody tells you this.
18. If a job has been "reposted" for 60 days, read this before you apply.
19. What recruiters mean when they say "we'll keep your resume on file."
20. Your resume isn't bad. It just doesn't match.

### Myth bust
21. Stop sending the same resume to every job.
22. A longer resume is not a better resume.
23. "Just network" is useless advice if you don't know anyone. Try this instead.
24. Cover letters aren't dead, but generic ones are.
25. 300 applications is free labor for 300 companies.
26. You're not unemployable. Your resume is just invisible.

### POV
27. POV: it's your 147th application and you're still saying "I'm excited about this opportunity."
28. POV: the job posting says "fast-paced environment" and you know what that means.
29. POV: you finally get a callback and it's a scam.
30. POV: you tailored your resume for 2 hours and they asked you to retype it into their form.
31. POV: the interviewer asks where you see yourself in 5 years and you're just trying to see yourself employed.

### Contrarian or curiosity
32. I stopped applying to jobs on Mondays. Here's why.
33. The best job I ever got, I almost didn't apply to.
34. Why I check one thing before I apply to anything.
35. The job market is broken. You're not.
36. Nobody talks about this part of job hunting.
37. I almost deleted my LinkedIn. Then this happened.

### Quick tips (value posts)
38. 3 words to delete from your resume today.
39. Do this in the first 48 hours a job is posted.
40. How to tell if a job posting is a ghost job in 10 seconds.
41. Copy this from the job posting into your resume. Seriously.
42. The one line every resume summary should have.
43. What to do when you get ghosted after an interview.
44. How to answer "what are your salary expectations" without losing money.
45. Your resume should change for every job. Here's the 5-minute version.

### Soft product (the 20%)
46. I used to spend my whole Sunday tailoring resumes. Now a Chrome extension points to what to change.
47. Before I apply anywhere, I see how I compare to the job first.
48. It points to real things I've done that the job wants. Nothing made up. My resume still sounds like me.
49. One click and the whole application filled itself. I'm not going back.
50. $99 for the year, no subscription, and I got my weekends back.

## 4. Voice cards

Each character rewrites the same hook in her or his own voice so 30 accounts never sound like one.

### Callie (live)
- Who: 24, recently graduated, figuring out the job market, sunny and a little chaotic.
- POV: "my friend / my roommate / me" storytime. Relatable, never preachy.
- Talks like: casual, short sentences, a bit dramatic for laughs. "be so for real," "I'm crying," "not me doing..."
- Humor: self-roasting, laughing at how absurd the job market is.
- Emoji: 😭 💀 🥲, one per caption, at the end.
- Never: corporate words ("leverage," "synergy"), hard selling, lecturing.
- Pillars: storytime, relatable pain, small wins, soft product.
- Example (hook 3): "entry level but they want 5 years experience. I'm 24. Be so for real 😭"

### Proposed: "The ex-recruiter" (to build)
- Who: early 30s guy, used to work in recruiting, now spills what really happens.
- POV: insider. "Here's what I saw behind the scenes."
- Talks like: calm, dry, confident, a little blunt. Short punchy lines.
- Humor: deadpan.
- Emoji: rarely, 👀 at most.
- Never: hype, "hustle" talk.
- Pillars: insider secrets, myth busts, ghost jobs, what recruiters really mean.
- Example (hook 19): "'We'll keep your resume on file.' I said that 400 times. There was no file 👀"

### Proposed: "The career switcher" (to build)
- Who: late 20s woman leaving a job she hates for a new field.
- POV: "I'm doing this in real time, come with me."
- Talks like: honest, encouraging, practical. Shares what she's trying.
- Humor: warm, a little sarcastic about corporate life.
- Emoji: ✨ 🙃
- Never: toxic positivity, fake success stories.
- Pillars: quick tips, process diaries, "what I'd do differently," soft product.
- Example (hook 45): "Your resume should change for every job. Here's how I do it in 5 minutes before work ✨"

## 5. Weekly rhythm (per batch of accounts)
- Day 1: pick 10 to 15 hooks, write variants per character voice.
- Day 2: make or reuse clips (reaction library, desk shots, lifestyle stills).
- Days 3 to 4: caption, edit, QA, schedule.
- Weekly: rank accounts by follows per post and signups per tracked link, not views. Clone what sells, pause what only gets views.
